# duelswarm — thesis, hypothesis, goal (the North Star the build serves)

Date: 2026-10-23
Status: canonical statement of what the range is for. Author: Dante. Every build decision serves
this; when engine correctness and a cleaner-looking result conflict, correctness wins, because the
range's only value is that it can be proven wrong.

## Thesis

You cannot read an autonomous agent's intent from the outside, and you cannot reliably say who is
behind an action. Rice's theorem is the wall behind the first: no checker can decide in general
what a program will do. In a world of autonomous AI agents this becomes the whole problem, because
that world is liquid. Both sides adapt. The attacker learns from the defender and the defender
learns from the attacker, each one's moves changing what the other becomes, so there is no stable
equilibrium to settle into and no detector whose edge the attacker cannot eventually climb. This is
reflexivity, and it is why detection loses once the cost of adaptation goes to zero.

The only thing that can hold in a liquid world is something that does not adapt and cannot be
adapted to: a deterministic floor that mediates every action against a fixed scope, and a
tamper-evident log that records what was done. Everything above them — the attacker, the defender,
the terrain, the strategy, the monitors — can be liquid. The floor and the log are the only parts
that do not move.

A correct floor does not make you safe. It cannot be fooled the way a detector is fooled, so an
attacker who studies it only learns the front door is shut, which moves the fight to two places: a
flaw in how the floor is built, and a chain of small allowed actions that add up to something the
floor was never meant to permit.

## Hypothesis

If the floor is real — every action mediated, every event hash-chained, the record re-derivable by
a checker that trusts neither the floor nor the agents — then whether any out-of-scope action
executed can be driven to zero and proven. That is a proof you write once, at the policy level,
not an experiment.

The experiment is what the non-reflexive floor does to the reflexive game above it. Four claims,
all open, to validate or invalidate:

1. The floor's shape acts as a selection pressure, steering what the adversary evolves into and
   shaping the adaptive defender in turn.
2. Under an adversary that never stops adapting, the floor does not break at the policy level;
   instead the attacker migrates to attacks assembled entirely from actions the floor allows.
3. The reason the floor can do this is informational: a monitor hands the attacker a gradient it
   can climb, while the floor hands it one bit and no gradient toward a catastrophic combination.
4. As floors multiply, the danger concentrates in the seams no single floor owns, where a real
   attack is a chain of individually allowed crossings that no single floor can see as a whole.

Only the policy-level guarantee — that nothing out of scope executes where the floor mediates — is
settled by construction. All four claims are open, and the point of the build is to get the data
that confirms or kills them.

## Goal

A sandboxed range where red and blue are both adaptive language models choosing their own gated
actions, with memory, so they genuinely co-evolve, all of it governed by the floor and the log.
Every statement the range produces is either re-derivable from the hash chain, scoped to a design
guarantee, or labeled pending. The range exists to watch what a fixed floor does to an adaptive
attacker and an adaptive defender, and to find where the harm goes once it can no longer go through
the front door.

The aim is not to assert that the floor wins. It is to build the one place where these claims can
be proven wrong.

## What this means for the build (the epistemic contract)

- A penetration is a RESULT, not a failed build. If the attacker reaches out-of-scope harm — by a
  flaw in how the floor is built, by a chain of allowed actions (claim 2), or through a seam
  (claim 4) — the range's job is to record it faithfully and show HOW, re-derivably. It is never
  discarded, prevented by fiat, or treated as a bug in the harness.
- What must hold by construction is the INSTRUMENT, not the attacker's failure: complete mediation
  (every agent action, including a self-authored tool, is dispatched through the one gate and
  written to the hash chain) and re-derivability by an independent checker. This is what makes a
  penetration attributable and provable.
- The one real build failure is an instrument failure: an effect that happens with no gate record,
  or a log that cannot be re-derived. An unrecorded effect cannot be studied, so it is the only
  thing the engineering must rule out — not the attacker getting through.
- The floor's egress parser is best-effort by design; the segmentation is the physical floor; the
  telemetry records what actually crossed the wire. Whether the floor holds under an adaptive,
  tool-authoring attacker is measured (e.g. `prove_env_boundaries` reports `oos_tcp_reaches`), not
  asserted.
